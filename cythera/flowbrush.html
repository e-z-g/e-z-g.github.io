Cycling registers

Delver animates exactly 28 indices — 0xE0–0xFB, the five ramps at the top of the list. That is all the cycling colour the game itself has.

You can allocate more from the rest of the CLUT. They cycle here and export correctly to GIF, but Delver will draw them as still paint, so leave them off if the art is bound for the game.

Switching one on takes those indices over: still paint already sitting in that range starts moving. That is what allocating a register means, and switching it back off settles it again.

How this works

A stroke stores position in a ramp, not colour. Every pixel holds one palette index. Inside a cycling ramp that index says how far along the ramp the pixel sits, and each frame rotates the ramp by one. So a stroke that writes a falling phase reads as colour travelling that way — drag down and it falls. Nothing is ever redrawn.

Band spacing is how many pixels one full turn of the ramp covers. Turbulence adds noise sampled in the stroke's own frame, so it stretches into streaks that run with the flow rather than speckling across it. Spiral arms winds the phase around the centre: zero gives concentric rings, anything else gives a vortex.

The palette is Cythera's — a 6-bit Mac CLUT widened to 8. The five engine ramps are the range the Delver engine actually rotates; the wiki records 0xE0–0xFB, and the split into fire, water, magic, earth and green is a reading of the palette rather than documented fact.

Exports. The GIF compresses the indices once and writes eight frames that differ only in their colour table, which is what palette cycling actually is. The PNG writes raw indices against the CLUT with index 0 transparent, so it drops straight into the game.
